Orientation

What this project is — and why it's worth it

You're turning an ordinary USB stick into a bootable, multi-tool field kit: a stick you can plug into almost any computer to repair it, scan it for malware, recover files, reset a forgotten password, image a disk, or boot into a full hacking distro — without installing a thing on the host machine.

🎒 It goes everywhere

Your whole toolkit rides in a pocket. Borrow any PC and you bring your environment with you — no admin rights on the host required for most of it.

🛠️ It's a real job skill

Boot media, offline malware removal, disk imaging and rescue are core help-desk and security tasks. Building this is the skill — not just reading about it.

🧩 It's modular

Five independent tools share one drive. You can add, remove, or update any one of them later without rebuilding from scratch.

The kit

The five tools — and when each one saves you

Each tool earns its slot on the drive. Here's what it is, why it's valuable, and a real scenario where you'd reach for it.

🩺

Medicat USB

A Ventoy-based bootable rescue suite — antivirus, diagnostics, partition tools, password reset, data recovery and a Windows PE environment, all bootable.

ScenarioA laptop won't boot or is infected. Boot Medicat, scan and clean it offline, then recover the user's files before reinstalling.
🐉

YUMI + Kali Linux

YUMI makes the drive multiboot; on it you install Kali Linux, the standard penetration-testing distro.

ScenarioYou need a full pentest toolset for an authorized lab on a machine that isn't yours — boot Kali from the stick, leave no trace on the host.
📦

PortableApps

Run real Windows apps — a hardened browser, antivirus scanners, utilities — straight from the USB. No install, often no admin rights needed.

ScenarioOn a locked-down public or lab PC, launch your own trusted browser and tools instead of whatever's on the host.
🌐

netboot.xyz

A tiny boot menu that pulls OS installers and tools over the network instead of from local ISOs (best when you self-host the files).

ScenarioInstall or rescue an operating system without carrying a pile of ISO files — fetch what you need on demand.
🧬

Clonezilla (“ghosting”)

Free disk-imaging and cloning. Capture a whole drive to an image, or clone a known-good image onto other machines.

ScenarioBefore a risky change, image the whole disk so you can restore it exactly — or deploy one image to many identical PCs.
🧠

Put together…

…these five turn one stick into a portable IT/security department: rescue, clean, recover, image, and assess — the exact toolkit a field technician carries.

Why it mattersReliable access to your tools on any machine, without depending on what's installed there.

Before you start — the ground rules

✅ You'll need

  • A USB drive 32 GB or larger (bigger is better — these tools are large).
  • A Windows PC to build from.
  • Patience: the Medicat download is big and slow on purpose.
  • All five installers downloaded ahead of time (links in Resources).

⛔ The golden rules

  • Identify your USB by size/label every time you select a drive.
  • During Medicat, keep “Toggle Drive Format” = No.
  • Download Medicat via CDN (direct), not torrent, on the school network.
  • Build in the correct order (next section) — it matters.
Partitions. Per the project instructions, your thumb drive should end up with exFAT and FAT partitions, and you use the YUMI exFAT installer to prepare it. YUMI is step 1 precisely because its first run formats and prepares the drive — after that, nothing else should format it.

The two screens people miss. (1) On the main menu, the red button must read “TOGGLE DRIVE FORMAT (CURRENTLY No)” — if it says Yes, click it once to flip it back. (2) In the Browse For Folder dialog, select your USB (shown as Ventoy (D:) in the example) — not your C: drive.

Step 1

YUMI exFAT + Kali Linux

YUMI prepares the drive and adds bootable distros. You'll put Kali Linux on it.

  1. Download YUMI exFAT from yumiusb.com (use the exFAT version, as the instructions specify).
  2. Download the Kali Linux “Live” ISO from kali.org first.
  3. Run YUMI. Step 1 — pick your USB (the screenshot shows E:\ (Disk 2) 232GB; choose yours).
  4. Step 2 — choose distribution: select Kali Linux (Penetration Testing).
  5. Step 3 — Browse to your kali…live….iso and select it.
  6. Let YUMI prepare the drive and copy Kali. On the first run it formats the drive to exFAT — that's expected because YUMI is step 1.
After YUMI, never format again. Every later tool (especially Medicat) must be told not to format, or you'll wipe the work you just did.

Helpful video: netboot.xyz on USB (covers multiboot setup).

Step 2

PortableApps

Portable Windows apps that run straight off the USB.

  1. Download the PortableApps.com Platform from portableapps.com.
  2. Run the installer and set the install location to your thumb drive (change the destination folder to the USB, not your C: drive).
  3. Pick the apps you want — it downloads and installs each one onto the drive (you'll see it work through them, e.g. “Downloading 4 of 19: Firefox…”).

netboot.xyz (Step 3) and Clonezilla (Step 5) round out the build. netboot.xyz needs you to self-host its files to be useful — follow the official USB docs. For Clonezilla, grab the stable release from clonezilla.org and add it to the drive.
